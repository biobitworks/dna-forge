EndoFORGE AI — hackathon pitch

Tagline: Connect the history. Contextualize the hormones. Trace the evidence.

TWO-MINUTE SCRIPT

Endometriosis research needs to connect symptoms across time with the biological
context in which measurements were taken. A pain score, a hormone measurement,
and a molecular sample tell different stories when separated from that timeline.

EndoFORGE AI is an explainable research workspace designed around primary care.
It brings clinical histories, hormone trajectories, transcriptomic features and
menstrual-blood research markers into one matched patient view. Endometriosis
and PCOS remain separate targets, so overlapping patterns can be examined.

The working local prototype uses a synthetic research cohort of 1,200 patients
with repeated visits, missing information and matched specimens. It shows two
research information cutoffs: an initial primary-care view and a later specialist
pre-biopsy view. Information collected later cannot leak into earlier scores.

Watch what happens when I remove an assay. The system uses a dedicated smaller
baseline when possible, rather than pretending the missing modality was observed.
We can inspect the contributing features, follow hormonal context, and export
a matched research cohort. Every calculation runs locally with open-source tools.

Our evaluation separates patients, preserves unknown outcomes and compares
combined models against clinical-only baselines on the same held-out patients.
Bootstrap intervals help show how stable these simulation results are. We do
not assume that adding modalities improves performance; the comparison tests it.

Today this demonstrates software, integration and evaluation feasibility on
synthetic data. It does not establish clinical diagnostic or referral accuracy.
The tissue experiment currently uses numeric surrogates, not a validated slide
model. Earlier identification is the research goal, not a demonstrated outcome.

Our next milestone is a clinician-led study with independently adjudicated
outcomes and matched samples, followed by external and prospective validation.
We are seeking a clinical and research-data collaborator to help define that
study and evaluate whether this evidence workspace improves the care pathway.

IF JUDGES ASK

What is new here?
The demonstrable integration combines patient/time matching, hormonal context,
separate overlap-aware outputs, modality-availability routing and transparent
model comparisons in a local workspace. We are not claiming a new validated
biomarker or a novel diagnostic algorithm.

Why use synthetic data?
It permits reproducible software and leakage testing without exposing patient
records. It cannot prove biological validity or clinical utility.

Does the combined model work better?
Not consistently in this simulation. We show that result rather than force an
improvement claim. The clinical baseline is an essential comparator.

What do the intervals mean?
They resample the fixed held-out synthetic patients for already-fitted models.
They do not capture training/generator uncertainty or clinical generalization.

Does this diagnose endometriosis?
No. It is a research prototype. Its targets, distributions and assay variables
are simulated. Clinical use requires independent validation and appropriate
clinical governance.

Do you have real histology/transcriptomics models?
Transcriptomic inputs are abstract simulated features. Tissue analysis uses
numeric surrogates only. Raw RNA processing and image-based pathology remain
future work; neither is represented as implemented clinical capability.

Avoid these claims: clinically validated, diagnoses early, reduces diagnostic
delay, real patient accuracy, validated biomarker, proven referral improvement.
